The Vision

Change how people consume — through games.

The founder's idea was ambitious: a gamified marketing platform where users earn points by playing games and redeem them for real products or discounts. Merchants pay to list products. Users earn, play, and shop. A closed loop between entertainment and commerce.

The product required 3D models, game logic, an integrated points system, a merchant backend, and a consumer-facing app — all from scratch, simultaneously. I was brought in as Project Lead: responsible for product direction, design management, team coordination, and investor communication. Everything except the capital allocation itself.

Campaign Creative

While the product was being built, I directed the brand's visual identity and social campaign materials — posters, promotions, and launch creatives.

The Build Story

18 months. One pivot. One costly lesson.

Months 1–3 · Kickoff

Scoping and local team assembly

Defined product requirements, hired a local Malaysian development firm. Game logic, 3D integration, merchant backend — the full scope was locked in. Timeline estimated: 9–12 months.

Months 3–5 · Crisis

Cost blowout — RM150K/month local burn rate

Local outsourcing costs hit RM10–20万 per month. At this rate, budget would be exhausted before delivery. An urgent call: find a cheaper path. Decision made to switch to a China development team.

Months 5–15 · Rebuild

China team onboarded — slower, but cheaper

Cost dropped significantly — but time zone friction, communication gaps, and the handoff process added 3–4 months. A 12-month plan became 15+. Quality had to be actively managed at distance.

Month 18 · Delivery

APP + backend system: fully delivered ✅

Both the consumer-facing app and the merchant backend were completed and launched. From a technical delivery standpoint — the project succeeded. Then reality arrived.

Post-Launch · The Problem

No users. No merchants. No ecosystem.

The chicken-and-egg problem hit immediately: users won't use an app with no merchants to redeem from. Merchants won't join a platform with no users. And the budget to solve this had already been spent on development.

Merchants

Platform businesses live or die by their cold-start problem. The strategy we needed — recruit 100 merchants first, give them a guaranteed user base, or recruit 1,000 users first and show merchants real demand — was never built into the roadmap. The plan was: build the app, then figure out acquisition. That sequence was the fatal mistake.

🔗

This is what led to Rezeki Gang

With the app complete but no users, the founder made a new decision: use TikTok content to build an audience first — at near-zero cost. That's how Rezeki Gang was born. 367K followers, 3M+ views. A free distribution engine, bootstrapped from the wreckage of an over-built app.

The Redo Playbook

What I'd do with 1/10th of the budget first.

1
H5 or mini-app MVP first — spend RM30K to validate the game mechanic, not RM300K on a full native app with 3D models nobody has played yet.
2
Recruit 100 merchants before writing code — commitments in hand, not optimism in a deck. If merchants won't sign a letter of intent, the product isn't ready to build.
3
Build TikTok audience in parallel from day 1 — not as a crisis response after launch, but as a deliberate acquisition channel running concurrently with development.
4
Validate the core loop with 1,000 test users — do they actually redeem? Do they invite friends? If yes, double down. If no, pivot before building the full stack.
5
Full app development only after loop is confirmed — invest the rest of the budget when unit economics are proven, not hoped for.
What I Learned

Technical delivery is not business success.

✓ What I Demonstrated

Led a complex product build across two international teams
Made tough cost-cutting decisions under live budget pressure
Managed remote China dev team across time zones
Delivered a functioning app + backend on a compressed budget
Pivoted strategy to TikTok when the original plan hit a wall

✗ What Was Backwards

Built the full product before validating demand — reversed priority
No cold-start plan for either users or merchants
Budget was for building, not for getting to first user — fatal gap
Estimated timeline was 9 months, delivered in 15 — 67% overrun
"Platform" with no ecosystem is just expensive software

"The app worked. The business didn't. A beautiful product with no distribution plan is just an expensive lesson in sequencing. Build the audience first, then the product — or at minimum, build them simultaneously. We did it backwards and paid in millions."
