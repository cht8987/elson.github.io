Lokes Ice Cream —
Made a brand
locals defend.

A new D2C ice cream entering a market full of noise.
The job wasn't just to make it look good — it was to make people feel something about it. Then tell their friends.
Here's how we got there.

The Challenge

A new brand in a crowded freezer.

Ice cream is one of the most emotionally driven categories in F&B — but also one of the most competitive. Lokes had a great product. What it didn't have was a reason for anyone to care yet. The brief: build a brand identity that makes people choose Lokes over every other option.

🏪
No brand recognition. Starting from zero in a market where customers already have go-to favourites.
📦
Packaging = first impression. In D2C, the box arriving at someone's door IS the experience. It had to earn a second order before they even tasted it.
📱
Social had to do the heavy lifting. No retail shelf space, no foot traffic — every discovery had to happen online.
🎯
The product needed a personality. Not just flavours and ingredients — a voice, a feeling, a reason to share.
The Approach

Brand first. Content second. Sales third.

The temptation with a new food brand is to lead with product shots and discounts. The better move is to build a world someone wants to belong to — and then invite them in. Lokes needed three things to land at the same time.

🎨

Brand Identity

Visual language, colour palette, typography, and a design direction that would work on packaging, social, and everything in between.

📦

Packaging Direction

The unboxing had to feel like a moment. Designed to get photographed and shared — earned media built into the product experience itself.

🗣️

Tone of Voice

Warm, playful, a little cheeky. A voice that made Lokes sound like a friend who happens to make very good ice cream — not a brand trying to seem relatable.

The Process

From blank page to launch content.

A brand launch isn't one deliverable — it's a sequence. Each step had to be done in order, because everything downstream depends on the decisions made upstream.

🔍

Brand Discovery

Deep dive into the founders' vision, the product story, and the target customer. What makes Lokes Lokes — before a single design decision gets made.

🎨

Visual Identity

Wordmark, colour palette, type system, and brand assets — built to scale across digital and physical. Warm, premium, approachable.

📦

Packaging Direction

Art direction for the product packaging — briefed to production with full context on brand intent. Built for the unboxing moment, not just the shelf.

✍️

Tone of Voice Guide

How Lokes speaks — captions, replies, stories, copy. A practical guide the team could actually use, not a 40-page brand bible no one reads.

🚀

Launch Content Series

Pre-launch teaser, launch week content, and first-month social calendar. Scripted, directed, and produced — built to introduce the brand and drive first orders.

What I Learned

What F&B brands actually need.

Lokes taught me a lot about how people relate to food brands specifically — and what separates brands people feel loyal to from ones they just try once.

01

Packaging is your biggest content piece.

In D2C, the box someone receives is the first physical interaction with your brand. When it's designed to be shared, your customers become your distribution channel. You don't need a big influencer budget if the product itself is worth photographing.

02

Tone of voice is a competitive advantage most brands underinvest in.

When the product quality is comparable, the brand that feels like a person wins. A consistent, distinctive voice builds familiarity faster than any logo redesign. And it's something a small brand can actually compete on against the big players.

03

Launch content is a sequence, not a single post.

A lot of brands treat the launch as one moment. The brands that grow treat it as a narrative — teaser, reveal, proof, community, repeat. Each piece builds context for the next one.
