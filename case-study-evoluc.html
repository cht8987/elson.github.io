The Setup

A platform to incubate brands. Built by me. Controlled by someone else.

I co-founded Evoluc Marketing with a business partner I'd known for nearly 10 years — a food entrepreneur with capital and an appetite for building new brands. His capital. My execution. A 33/67 equity split that sounded fair at signing and turned into the central tension of the entire venture.

The model: a design + sales + incubation platform. We'd build brands in-house, manufacture through third parties, sell through distribution channels. I was responsible for every operational layer — brand design, product planning, agent systems, social media, and daily execution. Everything except the final decision.

The Structural Problem

33% executive. 67% decisions.

He funded it. I built it. Neither fully owned the outcome.

On paper, this is a common arrangement. In practice, it means: every major call — new projects, budget allocations, strategic pivots — required alignment with someone who wasn't in the weeds. Decisions moved slowly. Momentum died in the gap between execution speed and approval speed.

What We Built

Everything. And nothing — deeply enough.

Evoluc ran simultaneous experiments across food, technology, and healthcare. Each brand I built from scratch: logo, packaging, website, agent system, social content. The capability was real. The focus was not.

The Traps

Why "doing everything" leads to nothing.

The Trap What Happened Outcome
Diversification New project every few months, each starting from zero None reached critical mass
Decision gap Needed partner sign-off; he was often unavailable or misaligned Strategy stalled repeatedly
Revenue model Tried client services (no profit), then brand incubation (burnt cash) No sustainable income base
Solo dependence All design, ops, sales ran through me — no system that worked without me Bottleneck in everything
Capital return Partner eventually recovered his principal from the projects Clean exit, no gain
The Exit

Three reasons. One moment.

I left Evoluc in 2024. Not because a single thing broke — but because three forces converged at once:

🧠 I wanted out

After three years of high execution with low autonomy, I needed to build something where I had real ownership — not just the title of CEO with no final say.

🔭 A better opportunity

The founder of Lokes Ice Cream invited me into the Heewon APP project — a larger, higher-stakes product play with a different structure and fresh energy.

⚡ The culture was broken

There were too many moments that didn't sit right — decisions made without me, credit not aligned with contribution, an atmosphere that became steadily harder to work in.

The company didn't collapse. It continued. But I left knowing that being titled CEO while holding minority equity and no veto power is functionally different from actually running a company — and that difference matters more than any revenue number.

What I Learned

The most important lessons aren't about business — they're about power.

✓ What I Built

Full-stack brand operating capability (design → sales → systems)
Multi-brand management across food, tech, healthcare simultaneously
Proven ability to go from zero to live brand in weeks
End-to-end agent system design and channel management

✗ What Failed

33/67 split = 0 effective decision power — never accept this again
Diversification is a trap in early-stage companies — focus one thing first
Confused "CEO" title with real founder authority — they are not the same
No revenue model that worked without burning capital

"Equity structure is destiny. A CEO with 33% who needs to ask permission is a senior employee with a nice title. Before the next venture, the first question I now ask is: who has the final say? If it's not me, I need to know exactly what I'm signing up for."
