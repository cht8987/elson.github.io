The Diagnosis

A lucky viral post isn't a strategy.

When I came in, the account had 160K followers from a single breakout video — but it had been stuck at that level for months. The first thing I did wasn't make content. It was pull data. Three months of content history, cross-referenced against growth data from the Rezeki Gang account I'd already built. The finding was clear.

Before
  • Random comedy — trending memes, no consistent identity
  • No data analysis — team didn't know why the viral video worked
  • Content made for the creator, not the audience
  • Product push interspersed randomly with entertainment
  • Growth flatlined at 160K for months
After
  • Street interview + interactive game formats — consistent identity
  • Bi-weekly data review — every decision backed by completion rate & shares
  • Framework-first content — audience-centric approach
  • Brand woven naturally into engaging formats
  • 244K followers, daily views restored to 50K–100K
The Strategy

Three formats. All audience-first.

The core shift: stop creating content the team found fun, and start creating content the audience wanted to watch and share. Three content pillars replaced the random posting schedule.

🎤

Random Street Interviews

Approach strangers near the store and in public spaces. Ask about ice cream preferences, brand awareness. Real reactions create natural content. "Would that be me?" keeps viewers watching.

Highest traffic
🎮

Interactive Games & Giveaways

Give away ice cream, cash challenges, mini competitions. People love watching others receive things — high entertainment value, strong brand association with generosity.

The real challenge wasn't content — it was people.

Some team members wanted to keep doing random comedy. My answer was data, not authority: "Let's run both approaches for two weeks and see what the numbers say." That conversation style — evidence over opinion — kept the team aligned without hierarchy friction. The lesson: in creative teams, the most persuasive thing isn't seniority. It's a good A/B test.

Being honest about what the numbers didn't show.

Followers grew. Daily views recovered. But sales conversion didn't move in measurable ways during the 6-month window. The gap between social growth and actual revenue is real — and building the bridge between them takes longer than one content cycle. The follower base was there. The content infrastructure was there. The conversion path was the missing piece. That's the honest read.

What I Learned

Managing people is harder than managing content.

01

Data beats hierarchy in creative disagreements.

When the team pushed back on the new direction, the answer was a 2-week test, not a command. Data from that test resolved the debate faster and cleaner than any argument about strategy could have. Build a culture where the numbers decide.

02

A "lucky viral post" is a warning, not an asset.

An account that grew on one unexplained breakout has no foundation — because no one knows why it worked. Before building on top of it, you have to figure out the mechanism. Otherwise you're building on luck.

03

Social growth and sales conversion are two different jobs.

Building an audience and building a revenue path both require dedicated work. 6 months was enough to fix the content engine. The sales funnel is a separate project — and one that deserves its own strategy from day one.
